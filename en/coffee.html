I

The Origin

Hambela lies in the Guji zone of southern Ethiopia, at 1,800–2,200 metres. In these highlands coffee grows densely and slowly — thinner air and cool nights give beans with a denser cell structure, higher sugar content and more pronounced acidity. The coffee is of the Heirloom variety, a collective term for the genetically diverse wild and semi-wild coffee trees unique to Ethiopia — the homeland of all Arabica coffee. The beans are harvested by several smallholders in the Hambela area and gathered into one controlled lot.

II

Natural

Natural processing — also called dry processing — means the whole coffee cherry is dried with the fruit left intact around the bean. During drying the bean absorbs sugars and aromas from the surrounding fruit. It is the process itself that gives the coffee its full body and juicy acidity — nothing added, no post-processing. Washed coffee is cleaner and more singular in expression. Natural processing is more complex and more demanding to do well.

III

Grade 1 and SCA 85

Grade 1 is the strictest class in the Ethiopian system and permits virtually no defects in the beans. It is the highest grading in the Ethiopian classification system and guarantees a homogeneous and almost flawless lot. The SCA score of 85/100 is awarded by certified Q graders following the Specialty Coffee Association cupping protocol. A score above 80 is defined as specialty coffee; above 85 is considered high quality on the global market. The score is not a marketing claim — it is a documented measurement of the raw product level.

IV

The Omni Profile

Omni means the roast level is adapted to work across brewing methods — espresso, drip and filter. A pure espresso profile is roasted darker to temper acidity and reinforce body; a pure filter profile is roasted lighter to bring out delicate aromas. Omni is the compromise: medium-light, where the natural-processed character from Hambela is retained without the coffee becoming too light for espresso. A good starting point for filter is 3–4 minutes of contact time. For espresso: 25–30 seconds of extraction at 93 °C.

V

Freshness

The coffee is roasted in small batches and sealed immediately. The roast date is hand-stamped on every bag. For the first 2–5 days after roasting the beans release CO2 — a natural process called degassing — and espresso will often give too much crema during this period. For filter you can brew after 24 hours. Best results are achieved between day 7 and day 45. Use within 3 months of the roast date for full character.
