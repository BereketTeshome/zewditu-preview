The Story

I

The Empress

Zewditu means “the crown” in Amharic. From 1916 to 1930 Empress Zewditu ruled Ethiopia — the same country the coffee comes from.

II

Guji

Guji is an Oromo zone in southern Ethiopia. High and cool. Here the coffee trees still grow semi-wild, closely related to the original types all Arabica descends from.

We could have chosen a cheaper origin. We could have blended several lots and got a more even, duller cup.

We chose Guji because the coffee from there has a distinct character of its own, and because one place and one lot can be traced all the way back. The global coffee industry has standardised most of that away. We wanted to go the other way.

III

Huaca Qumir

Huaca Qumir is a specialty coffee roastery in Moss. They roast in small batches and seal immediately. They usually trade directly with producers and stand as the responsible business under Norwegian food law.

IV

Zewditu Today

One product. One lot. One origin.

It is not a business strategy that looks impressive on paper, but it lets us do one thing properly instead of many things halfway. Every bag carries the roast date stamped by hand. Nothing sits in storage for months.

Joel Hayalu started Zewditu in 2026. Logo, label, brand and every step up to the shelf is built from the ground up.
